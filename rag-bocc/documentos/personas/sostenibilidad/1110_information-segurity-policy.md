                                    1. OBJECTIVE AND SCOPE
1.1 OBJECTIVE OF THE INFORMATION SECURITY AND CYBERSECURITY POLICY

This Information Security and Cybersecurity Policy is a statement of the responsibilities and accepted
conduct to maintain a secure environment at Banco de Occidente. This Policy establishes the guidelines
and directives related to the secure handling of information.

1.1.1 Principles

In order to fulfill its business objectives, Banco de Occidente has established the following principles as
fundamental, supporting the Information Security and Cybersecurity Policy:

• Information is one of Banco de Occidente’s most important assets and therefore it is expected to be
    used in accordance with business requirements.
• The confidentiality of business information and that of third parties must be maintained, regardless
    of the medium or format in which it is held.
• Business information must preserve its integrity regardless of its temporary or permanent residence,
    or the way in which it is transmitted.
• Business information must be available when required.
• The privacy of Banco de Occidente’s information must be preserved.

1.1.2 General Objective

The main objective of the Information Security and Cybersecurity Policy is for Banco de Occidente to
ensure that its information is accessed only by those who have a legitimate need for the performance
of their business functions (Confidentiality); that it is protected against unplanned modifications, made
with or without intent (Integrity); that it is available when required (Availability); and that it is used for the
purposes for which it was obtained (Privacy).

1.1.3 Specific Objectives

The specific objectives pursued by the Information Security and Cybersecurity Policy are:

• Establish the foundations for the development and implementation of the Information Security Model.
• Define the conduct to be followed regarding access, use, handling, and management of information
    resources.
• Establish and communicate responsibility in the use of information assets that support business
  processes and systems.
• Manage risks in Information Security and Cybersecurity.

• Establish communication channels that allow Senior Management to remain informed about risks
  and improper use of information assets, and the actions taken for their mitigation and correction,
  generating culture and commitment at all levels.
• Raise awareness among the Community about cybersecurity risks and ensure they have the
  necessary knowledge, skills, and technological means to maintain Information Security objectives.

• Protect the image, interests, and good name of Banco de Occidente.

This document provides the standards to support the Information Security and Cybersecurity Policies of
Banco de Occidente.

This Policy is part of Banco de Occidente’s Security Model under the responsibility of the Information
Security Leader, is reviewed and approved by the Board of Directors and the Information Security
Committee of Banco de Occidente, and is communicated to Information Owners, the Community, and
the different Information Security roles.

Changes that arise due to technological dynamics or for other reasons during the implementation of
these standards, and that impact the Information Security and Cybersecurity Policies, will be
documented and may lead to modifications of the Policy.

The Board of Directors of Banco de Occidente has at least one member with experience in information
assurance, which is aligned with the strategic oversight of Information Security and Cybersecurity.

Banco de Occidente is committed to the continuous improvement of its Information Security and
Cybersecurity framework through the ongoing enhancement of its ISMS, including the periodic review,
updating, and strengthening of controls, capabilities, and incident response processes, in line with the
evolving of risks, technological developments, and stakeholder expectations.


1.2 OBJECTIVES OF THE INFORMATION SECURITY STANDARDS

The Information Security standards of Banco de Occidente aim to develop the Information Security and
Cybersecurity Policy by formally documenting the rules for the protection of the Institution’s information
that are processed, transported, or stored through IT means such as software, hardware, networks, and
other associated facilities.

Through the application of the standards set out in this document, Banco de Occidente defines how
information is protected in a homogeneous manner and consistently with its criticality for the business,
across all IT resources.

1.2.1 Scope

The Information Security Standards respond to and develop the guidelines established by the
Information Security and Cybersecurity Policy and are required to implement a reliable and flexible
Information Security Model. This is supported by internal Information Security and Cybersecurity
procedures, whose objective is to document the main operational actions to be executed in order to
safeguard the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of information, maintaining the security levels
accepted by Banco de Occidente.

These standards are part of the Information Security Model and apply to all levels of the organization:
Users (including employees and shareholders), Clients, Third Parties (including suppliers and
contractors), Oversight Bodies, Related Entities, and Subsidiaries of Banco de Occidente, who access,
either internally or externally, any information asset that is stored, processed, or transported using
hardware, software, networks, and other associated facilities.

1.2.2 Compliance.

The standards must be complied with by all those who have access to or use Banco de Occidente’s
information through software, hardware, networks, and associated facilities.
Compliance with the standards is mandatory, and any exception must be documented as a risk incurred
by Banco de Occidente, which must be formally accepted by the Information Owner. The Community
must understand the implications of the standards and the responsibilities for their strict compliance.

Failure to comply with the standards may result in disciplinary or financial actions in the case of suppliers
or contractors, which may even lead to the termination of the contractual relationship and/or legal
actions. Ignorance of the standards does not exempt their application.

For new developments or new acquisitions of IT resources, the standards apply from their effective date.
A detailed plan must be established to adjust IT resources acquired prior to this effectiveness in order
to ensure compliance with the standards.

                                           2. GENERAL
2.1 POLICIES

2.1.1 Organization of the Policies

The document is fundamentally organized into two parts: the first describes the general objective of the
Information Security and Cybersecurity Policy, its characteristics, the responsible parties, and the way
in which it must be developed, implemented, and maintained. In the second part, individual policies
directly related to the general one are developed, specifying the conduct accepted by Banco de
Occidente in the handling of its information and the actions that must be taken to achieve the objectives
of this Policy.

2.1.2 Scope of the Policies

The Information Security and Cybersecurity Policy provides the guidelines required to implement a
reliable and flexible Information Security Model and defines the basic framework that will guide the
implementation of any standard, process, procedure, and/or action related to Information Security at
Banco de Occidente.

This Information Security and Cybersecurity Policy applies to all levels of the organization: Users
(including employees and shareholders), Clients, Third Parties (including suppliers and contractors),
Oversight Bodies, Related Entities, and Subsidiaries of Banco de Occidente that access, either internally
or externally, any information asset regardless of its location. Additionally, this Policy applies to all
information created, processed, or used in support of the business, and to employees’ personal
information, regardless of the medium, format, presentation, or location in which it is found.

2.1.3 Organization and Responsibilities

The administration of this Policy will be the responsibility of the Information Security Organization as
follows:

2.1.3.1 Information Security Committee

It is the executive group responsible for ensuring the planning, implementation, and maintenance of the
Information Security and Cybersecurity Policy, as well as the execution and timely reporting to Senior
Management of the actions required to maintain the established security levels.

2.1.3.2 Information Security Leader

This role is part of the second line of defense and must establish the guidelines in this area and
continuously monitor compliance with all Information Security and Cybersecurity Risk obligations.

The Information Security Leader is also responsible for presenting management results directly to
Senior Management or the Audit Committee. This role must have sufficient resources to effectively
perform all functions and play a central and proactive role in the Information Security Management
System. To do so, they must be fully familiar with current policies and standards, their legal and

2.1.3.3 Cybersecurity Leader

This is the executive responsible for ensuring the secure operation of the Organization’s technological

through business-enabling Cybersecurity strategies and proactively defend the Organization against
cyber threats, preventing the occurrence and recurrence of cybersecurity incidents.

2.1.3.4 Information Owner

This is the executive for whom the information was created in order to carry out their business and is

implement the Information Security and Cybersecurity Policy within their area, and to do so, they must

2.1.3.5 Community

They are responsible for protecting Banco de Occidente’s information assets by complying with the
Information Security and Cybersecurity Policy. Likewise, they must remain alert to identify and report
any non-compliance or breach of established standards or procedures.

Banco de Occidente will define the roles and responsibilities required to complete the definition of the
Information Security organization, always promoting the segregation of duties as a method to reduce
risks in the misuse of information.

2.1.4 Audit

The disclosure of information related to internal audit processes in the Management Report, as part of
the third line of defense, plays a fundamental role by enabling the independent and periodic evaluation
of the management and controls of information security and cybersecurity risks, as well as the policies,
standards, and system procedures. This process is carried out through the disclosure and explicit
formalization of periodic internal audits of the IT infrastructure and the information security management
system, which are executed in accordance with the annual audit plan or based on the behavior of IT
and information security risks.

2.1.5 Compliance and Management of Policy Violations

Compliance with the Information Security and Cybersecurity Policy and its respective standards is
mandatory; each member of the Community must understand their role and assume responsibility
regarding Information Security risks and the protection of Banco de Occidente’s information assets. Any
breach of this Policy that compromises the Confidentiality, Integrity, Availability and/or Privacy of
information will result in disciplinary action, which may include termination of the employment contract
and a possible initiation of legal proceedings under applicable national or international laws.

The Information Security and Cybersecurity Policy is based on best security practices and is aligned
with national and international legislation; therefore, it will take the necessary steps, including applicable
legal measures, to protect its assets and their use.

2.1.6 Administration of Change Policy and Procedure

The Information Security and Cybersecurity Policy is expected to be maintained over time. However, it
must be reviewed annually or when structural changes affecting Banco de Occidente occur, to ensure
it meets evolving business needs. The Information Security Leader is responsible for this task and must
carry it out considering Corporate and Bank guidelines.

Any member of the Community may identify the need to modify the Information Security and
Cybersecurity Policy; such concerns and suggestions must be communicated to the Information
Security and Cybersecurity Leader, who is responsible for its maintenance.

If an addition or change to the Policy is required, the Information Security and Cybersecurity Leaders
will carry it out and submit it for approval to the Board of Directors and the Bank’s Information Security
Committee. The formalization of the new Information Security and Cybersecurity Policy will be carried
out by the Information Security Leader.

2.1.7 Exceptions to the Policy

There are no exceptions to this Policy.

2.1.8 Policy Implementation and Program

The Information Security and Cybersecurity Policy involves the development and implementation of a
broad Information Security program integrated into the day-to-day operations of Banco de Occidente.
An effective Information Security program is a continuous process, not an event. To achieve the
objectives set forth in this document, this Policy anticipates and authorizes the development of
standards, detailed operational procedures, and other administrative measures; as well as the
development or acquisition of preventive and reactive mechanisms, software tools, and other protection
techniques.

2.1.9 Cybersecurity Risk Management Framework

The Cybersecurity risk management framework establishes a system to identify, analyze, assess,
mitigate (treat), monitor, and communicate associated cyber risks in a way that enables Banco de
Occidente to mitigate the likelihood of occurrence, the materialization of risk, and its impact. The
Cybersecurity risk management system is an integral part of the corporate SARO methodology.

2.1.10.1 Information Security

Policy: Business information is a vital asset of Banco de Occidente and must therefore be
protected.

Banco de Occidente’s information, regardless of its representation, medium, or format, in which it is
created or used to support business activities, is classified as business information or an information
asset.

Banco de Occidente must have the necessary means to ensure that each member of the Community

to disable, bypass, or override any security control will be subject to immediate disciplinary action.

Business Information Security is the set of protection measures taken by Banco de Occidente against

Information Owners are responsible for ensuring that business information has appropriate protection

2.1.10.2 Cybersecurity

Policy: Cybersecurity is oriented toward the development of capabilities and practices to defend
against and anticipate cyber threats.

Banco de Occidente must have the necessary means to ensure information supported by IT systems
by developing business capabilities and practices to defend against and anticipate cyber threats, in
order to protect and secure data, systems, services, and applications that are essential to the business
across internal corporate networks and in cyberspace.

2.1.10.3 Intellectual Property

Policy: Ownership of information must be maintained.

Intellectual Property is defined as any patent, copyright, invention, or information that is the property of
Banco de Occidente.

All material developed while working for the Bank is considered its intellectual property and is for its
exclusive use; therefore, it must be protected against disclosure, discovery, or use that undermines the
Bank’s competitiveness.

2.1.10.4 Information Owners

Policy: Each information asset of Banco de Occidente must have an owner who is responsible
for its security.

Banco de Occidente uses information to carry out its activities. This information is created and assigned
to each executive so they can develop and fulfill their respective goals within the framework of the
business.

The information that the Bank uses to achieve its business objectives must have an assigned owner,
who uses it within their area and is responsible for its proper use. Therefore, they are in charge of making
the decisions required to protect their information and determining who the users are and their usage
privileges.

Policy: Banco de Occidente must comply with local and international Privacy and Information
Security regulations.

The Information Security and Cybersecurity Policy is aligned with and supports compliance with local

must be included in the development of the Security Model, and specific actions must be taken to keep

Likewise, and to maintain a good level of security, this Policy must be supported by Information Security

2.1.10.6 Information Security and Cybersecurity Risk Management

Policy: The risks to which Banco de Occidente’s information is exposed must be identified,
assessed, and mitigated according to their value, likelihood of occurrence, and impact on the
business.

Business information must be protected based on its value and the risk to which it may be exposed.
Therefore, a high-level analysis of business impact in Information Security must be conducted
periodically to determine or update the relative value of the information, the level of risk to which it is
exposed, and the respective Information Owner.

Once the level of risk and the value of the information are established, each Information Owner must
carry out a formal risk assessment so that risks are identified, evaluated, and necessary actions are
implemented to address or mitigate them according to the risk levels permitted by the Bank.

Each user of the information must be aware of procedures for reporting risks that could impact the
Bank’s Information Security and is required to immediately report any suspicion or observation of an
incident to Information Security.

2.1.10.7 Training and Culture Building in Information Security and Cybersecurity

Policy: Banco de Occidente must establish an ongoing program to build a culture of Information
Security and Cybersecurity for users and third parties.

Banco de Occidente must have a permanent program to ensure that users and third parties are informed
about their responsibilities in Information Security and the ongoing threats that put the information they
handle at risk.

Users and third parties must be aware of the Information Security procedures they must apply in addition
to those required to perform their job functions. As part of the training program, new personnel must
attend, during the induction period, a session on the Bank’s Information Security and Cybersecurity
requirements..

Policy: Banco de Occidente must provide the necessary mechanisms to ensure that its
employees fulfill their Information Security responsibilities from hiring to termination.

New employees joining Banco de Occidente or those changing positions must undergo an investigation
process to mitigate risks in the use of business information. The degree of investigation will depend on
the level of contact they will have with the information.

Employee contracts must include clauses specifying the care that must be taken with the Bank’s

regarding Information Security and compliance with the code of conduct must be clearly defined, making
them aware of the consequences in case of non-compliance. A record of each employee’s
understanding and adherence to the Information Security and Cybersecurity Policy must be maintained.

The Bank will develop a program for handling suggestions in Information Security and Cybersecurity,
through which employees will report vulnerabilities and risks they detect. Likewise, personal work
objectives must include goals related to compliance with this Policy.

2.1.10.9 Third Parties Accessing Banco de Occidente Information Locally or Remotely

Policy: Third Parties that use Banco de Occidente’s information locally or remotely must comply
with the Information Security and Cybersecurity Policy.

The use of Banco de Occidente’s information by Third Parties, Related Entities, and Subsidiaries,
whether locally or remotely, must be formalized through agreements that make compliance with this
Policy mandatory. Such personnel must be investigated according to their level of contact with the
information. Service contracts must include a formal Information Security Service Level Agreement
detailing their commitments regarding the care of the Bank’s information and the penalties they would
face in case of non-compliance. Compliance with these Information Security Service Levels must be
verified periodically.

Each relationship with a Third Party, Related Entity, or Subsidiary must have a high-level representative
within the Bank who ensures the proper use and protection of business information. This person will be
responsible for the activities of such personnel during the term of the contract.

2.1.10.10 Individual Identification and Authentication

Policy: All members of the Community accessing Banco de Occidente’s information must have
a means of identification, and access must be controlled through personal authentication.

Each member of the Community is responsible for their actions while using any information resource of
Banco de Occidente. Therefore, the identity of each member accessing IT resources must be uniquely
established and authenticated and must not be shared.

Members of the Bank’s Community will be required to authenticate themselves before gaining access
to information. Depending on the value of the information and the level of risk, the Bank will define
appropriate authentication methods, which must not be shared (such as passwords) and must only be
enabled during the Bank’s established working hours. These authentication means contain confidential
information that must not be disclosed or stored in places accessible to unauthorized persons.

2.1.10.11 Control and Administration of Access to Information

Policy: The use of Banco de Occidente’s information must be controlled to prevent unauthorized
access. Privileges over information must be maintained in accordance with business needs,
limiting access only to what is required.

Mechanisms for physical and logical access control must be established to ensure that information
assets are protected consistently with their value to the business and the risks of loss of Confidentiality,
Integrity, Availability, and Privacy.

Access rights must not compromise the segregation of duties and responsibilities. Access to the Bank’s
information must be granted only to authorized users, based on what is required to perform their
responsibilities. Access must be restricted in all cases, following the principles of need-to-know and
least privilege.

2.1.10.12 Information Classification

Policy: Information Owners must classify information based on its value, sensitivity, risk of loss
or compromise, and/or legal retention requirements.

Like other assets, not all information has the same use or value and therefore requires different levels
of protection. All Banco de Occidente information will be classified by the Information Owner based on
a high-level analysis of business impact in Information Security, determining its relative value and level
of risk.

Each classification level will have a set of controls defined by the Information Owner and the Information
Security Leader to ensure appropriate and consistent protection throughout the Bank, regardless of
medium, format, or location. These controls must be applied throughout the information lifecycle.

2.1.10.13 Business Continuity

Policy: All information resources and mission-critical processes must have a Business
Continuity Plan, a Disaster Recovery Plan, and be prepared for Information Security and
Cybersecurity attacks.

Information must be available for authorized use when required. Procedures must be developed,
documented, implemented, and periodically tested to ensure timely recovery of critical information.

The Bank will establish immediate response measures to detect and mitigate the effects of Cyber attacks
against Information Security, such as denial-of-service and the introduction of unauthorized code. These
measures will be based on procedures and elements that allow the Bank to remain informed about the
existence of these threats, detect attacks immediately, and execute the corresponding actions.

2.1.10.14 Physical Security

Policy: All physical business areas must have a level of security consistent with the value of the
information processed and managed within them. Confidential or business-sensitive information
must be kept in restricted-access locations when not in use.

The physical security of Banco de Occidente must be based on perimeters and secure areas, which will
be protected through appropriate surrounding controls. These must be consistent with the value of the
information they contain, and minimum access rights must be granted considering whether the
workplaces are permanent or not.

The Bank’s IT resources must be physically protected against unauthorized access threats and

Information classified as confidential or restricted must not be left unattended or uncontrolled; therefore,
the Bank will develop a program to prevent critical business information from being accessed without
authorization.

2.1.10.15 Non-Repudiation

Policy: The authenticity of any business or electronic transaction carried out by Banco de
Occidente must be ensured.

Banco de Occidente increasingly relies on electronic means to conduct its business. Therefore, for any
business or transaction carried out through these means, the Bank must ensure the authenticity of each
involved party and prevent any of them from denying their participation (non-repudiation).

When conducting electronic business, traceability must be generated to allow the Bank to resolve
conflicts when any party denies participation. These must be generated, stored, and accessed in
accordance with the Bank’s internal Policies and local law.

2.1.10.16 Alert Management

Policy: Banco de Occidente must be alerted at the exact moment when violations of the
Information Security and Cybersecurity Policy occur.

Situations or actions that violate this Policy must be detected, recorded, and immediately reported to
Senior Management (alerts). An incident management program must be developed that prioritizes such
alerts and resolves them according to the criticality of the information for the Bank. Such program must
include the definition of an immediate response organization to address these and other situations that
the Bank considers critical.

2.1.10.17 Auditability of Information Security Events

Policy: Banco de Occidente’s Information Security records must be continuously reviewed to
ensure compliance with the Information Security Model.

Information Owners must define events considered critical and the respective Information Security
records that must be generated. These must be activated, stored, and continuously reviewed, and
unexpected situations must be reported in a timely manner to the Information Owners, as well as to the
required levels. Records and the means that generate and manage them must be protected by controls
that prevent unauthorized modification or access, to preserve the integrity of the evidence.

2.1.10.18 Connectivity

Policy: All connections to public networks must be authenticated to prevent information from
being disclosed or altered.

Connections to Banco de Occidente’s private network must be carried out securely to preserve the
confidentiality, integrity, availability, and privacy of the information transmitted over the network.
Likewise, all outgoing access to other companies must be conducted over networks approved by the
Bank.

Members of the Community connecting to the private network must comply with this Policy before the
connection is made. This also applies to any current or future connection within the Bank’s network that
uses public means to integrate geographically dispersed locations.

Approval from the Information Owner is required to remotely access the Bank’s information, and such
access must comply with the Identification and Authentication Policy.

2.1.10.19 Use of IT resources for busines

Policy: IT resources are provided to the Community for exclusive business use.

Banco de Occidente’s IT resources are exclusively for business purposes and must be treated as assets
dedicated to providing the tools required to perform the work. Members of the Community who attempt
to access information for which they do not have an authorized business requirement are violating this
Policy.

When using the Bank’s information, privacy should not be assumed; therefore, when it is used, records
of the activity performed must be created, which may be reviewed in order to detect abuses and threats.
If this is the case, the corresponding procedures will be executed in accordance with the Bank’s
regulations.

The Bank reserves the right to restrict access to any information at any time it deems appropriate.
Selected personnel of the Bank may use restricted-use technology such as network monitoring,
operational data, and Information Security event monitoring. No unauthorized hardware or software may
be loaded, installed, or activated on IT resources without prior formal authorization from the Information
Security Leader.

Bank’s system administration processes in order to comply with the Information Security and
Cybersecurity Policy.

2.1.11 Glossary for the Security Model Policies

The following definitions are some of the concepts used within the Information Security and
Cybersecurity environment with which Community members must be familiar:

• Controls: Safeguards based on devices or mechanisms required to comply with Policy requirements.

• Information resources: All categories of information, including but not limited to records, files, and
  databases, and (2) information technology facilities, equipment (including personal computing
  systems), and proprietary or licensed software of Banco de Occidente.

• Information Owner: An individual or organizational unit that has responsibility for classifying and
  making control decisions regarding the use of its information.

• Physical security: Protection of information processing equipment from physical damage,
  destruction, or theft; protection of facilities assigned for information processing from damage,
  destruction, or unauthorized access; and protection of personnel from potentially harmful situations.

• Cybersecurity: Development of organizational capabilities to defend against and anticipate cyber
  threats in order to protect and secure data, systems, and applications in cyberspace that are essential
  for the entity’s operations.

• Risk: The probability that an Information Security event will occur, causing loss to Banco de
  Occidente.

                                    3. OPERATING PROCEDURE

The security management processes mentioned in the Cybersecurity Standards are:

3.1 USER IDENTIFICATION AND AUTHENTICATION

Identifies and verifies the identity of users and programs accessing systems.


3.2 ACCESS CONTROL

It protects computer resources by ensuring that they can only be used by authorized individuals or
programs. Access control techniques can be used to protect a variety of resources including hardware,
operating systems, communications centers, programs, and files or databases.


3.3 AVAILABILITY

 Ensures that all system facilities, including security services, are available as required for business
applications and system infrastructure.


3.4 CONFIDENTIALITY

It ensures that information controlled by the system is not used or accessed in violation of access control
policies. Confidentiality mechanisms can protect the content of communications, the contents of files
and databases, programs, program tables, and other files required by the system. Encryption techniques
are used to provide confidentiality.


3.5 INTEGRITY OF INFORMATION

It ensures that the information in any form has not been modified either inadvertently or deliberately.
Integrity mechanisms also make it possible to recognize when the integrity of a resource has been
violated.

Verify that the security controls that were established for the operation of the computer resources are

3.7 PHYSICAL SECURITY

It involves the physical protection of resources such as office work areas, desks, computers, and
communication lines. Physical resources are exposed to risks created by accidental and deliberate
threats.


3.8 CERTIFICATION OF INFORMATION

It demonstrates that the communication of the information indicated as having been sent to a recipient
was, in fact, sent and received, and that the communication of the information indicated as having been
received from a sender was, in fact, issued by the sender.


3.9 ALERT MANAGEMENT

Notify security managers and resource owners when security breaches occur. The most effective
security alert processes automatically notify the appropriate party as soon as a security breach occurs.
A security alert should result in the initiation of an analysis, review, and, if necessary, disciplinary
proceedings.


3.10 AUDITABILITY MANAGEMENT

Review safety-related practices and activities. Review the security privileges granted to users of the
system, the information files related to the security logs that are recorded as the system is used, and
the manual and automatic procedures related to Information Security.


3.11 SECURITY MANAGEMENT

 It involves the ongoing activities of security administrators who implement security policies, standards,
and procedures using a variety of manual and automated techniques.


3.12 POLICY MANAGEMENT

It defines the security policies that will be managed, published as part of the security awareness process,
and executed with manual and automated procedures.
