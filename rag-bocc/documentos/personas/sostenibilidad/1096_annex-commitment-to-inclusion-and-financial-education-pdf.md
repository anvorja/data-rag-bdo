                         Commitment to Financial Inclusion and Education

In line with our corporate purpose of generating long-term shared value and contributing to
inclusive economic development, Banco de Occidente has voluntarily adopted the Asobancaria
Social Protocol, a self-regulatory framework that promotes responsible, equitable, ethical and
inclusive banking practices within the Colombian financial sector.

This Protocol recognizes the financial sector as a key enabler of sustainable development and
inclusive growth, establishing principles aimed at:

    •   Expand financial access for vulnerable and traditionally underserved populations.
    •   Design financial products and services focused on customer needs.
    •   Strengthen users' financial and digital capabilities.
    •   Reduce structural and operational barriers to access.
    •   Promote an organizational culture based on equity and inclusion.

This annex summarizes how Banco de Occidente materializes these commitments through
measurable initiatives, governance mechanisms, scope indicators, and continuous improvement
actions aimed at strengthening results in financial inclusion.

1. Introduction

This complementary document consolidates the Bank's main initiatives and commitments in the
area of financial inclusion and education, aligned with the principles established in Asobancaria's
Social Protocol.

Banco de Occidente adheres to a sectoral commitment framework that promotes the financial
inclusion of historically underserved populations, such as women, rural populations, people with
disabilities, migrants, and other vulnerable groups. This Protocol establishes guidelines for financial
institutions to expand and adapt their offer of financial products and services based on market
studies, identification of barriers and specific needs of these populations, as well as the design of
relevant financial and non-financial solutions. It also promotes the development of financial
education programs and the strengthening of economic capacities that contribute to financial well-
being and better decision-making by users.

In addition, the Protocol promotes the implementation of differentiated and inclusive care models,
through accessible channels, reasonable accommodations, and service strategies adapted to the
characteristics of each population group. It also promotes the training of human talent in inclusive
practices, appropriate language and the elimination of bias in commercial service, as well as
articulated work with public and private actors to expand the scope of financial inclusion and
strengthen the sector's ecosystem. This commitment is promoted by a sectoral governance body
led by Asobancaria, through a committee that guides, monitors and promotes the implementation
of the Protocol among its signatory entities.

Adherence to this Protocol represents a formal institutional commitment to promote equitable
access to financial services, drive responsible and inclusive banking solutions, and strengthen

6. Responsible Credit Practices and Prevention of Over-Indebtedness

Banco de Occidente has a comprehensive credit risk management framework aimed at preventing
situations of over-indebtedness, particularly in target segments and potentially vulnerable
populations. The credit origination processes incorporate evaluations of payment capacity and
financial affordability, considering variables such as cash flow generation, financial burden, income
stability, leverage level and comprehensive financial profile of the customer, under the internal
methodology of the "5 C's of Credit".

For personal banking products, all applications are evaluated using credit scoring models, validations
in credit bureaus and financial behavior analysis, including mandatory consultations with CIFIN and
Datacrédito, which allows the identification of high levels of indebtedness, deterioration in payment
habits and risks of overexposure before credit approval.

In addition, the Bank implements exposure limits, debt thresholds and risk concentration controls
at both the individual and portfolio levels, aligned with the Credit Risk Management System (SARC)
and the Risk Appetite Framework (MAR). Credit exposure is continuously monitored through early
warning indicators, periodic portfolio reviews and follow-up mechanisms supervised by the Financial
Risk Committee and the Board of Directors.

When impairments in financial capacity or adverse changes in client conditions are identified, the
Bank may restrict additional disbursements, reassess exposure levels, adjust approved quotas, or
implement risk mitigation measures in order to prevent over-indebtedness scenarios. These
practices are complemented by financial education initiatives and responsible credit principles
incorporated within the framework of corporate governance and financial consumer protection.
