# human rights due diligence

<!-- página 1 -->

                                          Due Diligence Report
                                          in Human Rights 2025


At Banco de Occidente, we work in coordination with the different areas of the organization to
integrate environmental, social, and governance criteria into our operations, ensuring that each
decision has a positive impact on the people and environment where we operate. In this context,
respect for the Human Rights of our stakeholders is consolidated as a cross-cutting axis of our
sustainability strategy, allowing us to responsibly manage relationships throughout our value chain.

Based on international standards such as the United Nations Guiding Principles on Business and Human
Rights, as well as the applicable regulations in Colombia and the guidelines of the Aval Group, the
Bank has been progressively strengthening our human rights due diligence process, evolving from an
initial stage of risk identification and assessment to an implementation phase. monitoring and
continuous improvement.

During 2025, we made progress in the structured monitoring of the mitigation plan defined in the 2024
due diligence exercise, consolidating periodic monitoring mechanisms, articulation with the
responsible areas, and strengthening existing controls for the management of risks and human rights
impacts.

In a complementary manner, and as part of our institutional maturity process, we began the design of
a Management System for Human Rights Due Diligence, whose development covers the second half
of 2025 and the first half of 2026. This system aims to allow autonomous, systematic and traceable
management of human rights risks, integrating this analysis into the organization's existing risk
architecture, controls and processes, based on the corporate guidelines of the Aval Group.

This model seeks to incorporate a cross-cutting human rights approach into the Bank's operations,
facilitating the early identification of risks based on existing signals, events, and grievance mechanisms,
as well as the timely activation of prevention, mitigation, and remediation measures. It will also
strengthen governance, the assignment of responsibilities and the traceability of management,
ensuring its alignment with the United Nations Guiding Principles and the guidelines of the Aval Group.

In this regard, the present report provides an account of the progress achieved during 2025 in the
implementation of mitigation plans addressing the identified risks, as well as the advances made in
consolidating a structured due diligence approach that will continue to be strengthened in the coming
cycles.


Human Rights Due Diligence Process

Our human rights due diligence approach extends to the entire organization, including our operations,
suppliers, and business partners. To this end, we carry out systematic and periodic reviews that allow
us to map the main challenges and establish concrete measures for the prevention, mitigation and
remediation of negative impacts.

For the implementation of Human Rights Due Diligence for Banco de Occidente, in 2024 we had the
support of the firm PWC and we carried out the following phases:

Context analysis from two perspectives: we reviewed 15 international standards and documents
related to the relationship between companies and human rights, as well as three (3) colombian
regulatory instruments; additionally, we examined banking sector dynamics and consulted six (6)
external organizations in order to understand their perception of the Bank’s management and its
potential human rights risks and impacts.


<!-- página 2 -->

Furthermore, we reviewed the Bank’s internal processes and their connection to respect for human
rights, analyzing 39 corporate governance documents. This analysis was complemented by 14
interview sessions with areas potentially related to human rights issues.

Finally, 8 key relevant issues were identified, which served as input for the following phases of the
process:

             •   Comprehensive management and transparency in information.
             •   Decent working conditions and Health and safety at work.
             •   Diversity, equity and inclusion.
             •   Responsible Social Investment and Environmental Protection and Preservation
             •   Personal and family well-being of workers.
             •   Habeas data and use of personal data.
             •   Accessibility to digital channels.
             •   Freedom of association and access to collective bargaining.


Identification of risks and impacts:

We map the possible risks and impacts on Human Rights within our operations and value chain, through
the correlation of primary and secondary sources: benchmarking with 17 external sources to recognize
the risks and impacts on Human Rights that concern the financial sector, and 40 consultation spaces
for the collection of information with the different stakeholders of the Bank. which include interviews,
surveys (collaborators, NGOs and foundations, guilds, suppliers and business partners, regulators and
authorities, business clients and individuals). Finally, the information obtained was contrasted with the
information collected in the benchmark, resulting in 21 possible risks and negative impacts on human
rights for the Bank's operation and our supply, of which 8 are impacts, 8 are risks and 5 are perceived
risks.


<!-- página 3 -->

During the first human rights due diligence cycle in 2024, the Bank prioritized the risks identified as most
relevant to the operation. Issues such as child labor, forced labor, and human trafficking were not
included in the initial risk matrix, because they were addressed through the Code of Ethics and
AML/CFT prevention policies.

In line with international best practices, in the following due diligence cycles we will explicitly
incorporate these issues into the identification and assessment of risks, even when they are of low
probability, to ensure their permanent monitoring.

On the other hand, the analysis was carried out considering broad interest groups, such as employees,
customers and suppliers. While these groups include diverse populations (women, men, and different
profiles), the process did not systematically incorporate a differentiated analysis for potentially more
vulnerable populations such as women, children, indigenous communities, or migrant workers.

As part of continuous improvement, the Bank will advance in the progressive incorporation of a
differentiated approach that allows the identification of specific risks for these groups and the definition
of proportional actions where appropriate.


Risk and impact assessment and action plan:

Once the process of identifying risks and negative impacts on human rights has been completed, we
carry out the assessment of these following the main international standard in the field, the United
Nations Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights (UNGPs), the Guide for the Assessment and
Management of Human Rights Impacts (EGIDH) of the Global Compact and the ranges used by the
organization's Risk area.

These criteria allow the Bank to be clear on the following:

•   Scale: to measure the level of impact on rights holders.
•   Scope: number of people affected in the Bank's area of influence and stakeholders.
•   Irremediability: the Bank's ability to return rights holders to the situation prior to the impact, through
    prevention, mitigation, and remediation actions.

Additionally, we evaluate the occurrence through past events, giving a rating from 1 to 5 according
to the frequency that the impacts have had on stakeholders; and the type of involvement in
determining whether the Bank causes, causes, or contributes to, such risks and impacts.

Once the assessment is completed, we prioritize the most serious and urgent risks and impacts on
human rights to manage according to the level of severity, occurrence and level of involvement;
which gives way to the construction of the mitigation and remediation plan.

Based on the work carried out in 2024, in 2025 we carry out a periodic structured monitoring of the risk
and impact map, through quarterly spaces with the responsible areas, allowing us to monitor progress,
adjustments and effectiveness of controls.

Mitigation and remediation plans implemented

Mitigation and remediation actions are classified as: i) preventive, such as policies, training and
controls; ii) corrective measures, which include process adjustments and disciplinary sanctions; and iii)
remediation, such as reparation of damages, restitution of rights and compensation when applicable.

The Bank maintains an evaluation of 100% of its own operations, as well as its relationship with suppliers
and other actors in the value chain, identifying and prioritizing human rights risks based on criteria of
severity, occurrence and involvement.

Banco de Occidente and its subsidiaries maintain a permanent commitment to strengthening the
mechanisms for participation, dialogue and complaints of its stakeholders, with the aim of ensuring
effective management of the remediation of human rights impacts.

Through these mechanisms, stakeholders can submit their requirements or complaints, in an identified
or anonymous manner, which are managed in accordance with established procedures,
guaranteeing confidentiality, traceability and compliance with applicable legal deadlines.

As a relevant milestone in 2025, the Bank explicitly incorporated the human rights approach into the
Ethics Line, allowing the specific classification of complaints associated with possible violations in this
area. This advance strengthens the institutional capacity to identify, analyze, and manage human
rights risks, improving the traceability of cases and the quality of monitoring and reporting. In this
context, during 2025 there were no complaints associated with possible human rights violations through
this channel, which reflects the trend observed in reporting mechanisms, without prejudice to the
actions taken to strengthen the identification, classification, and management of this type of situation
in institutional channels.

Communication and transparency: The Bank reports on the progress of its human rights management
through different mechanisms, including:

    •   The disclosure of policies, procedures and results in the Management and Sustainability Report,
        as well as in the Human Rights Report.

    •   Periodic monitoring of the action plan, with a quarterly report to the Steering Committee.

    •   The promotion of spaces for dialogue with stakeholders to strengthen accountability and
        participation.

Review and continuous improvement: The Bank adapts its due diligence process based on lessons
learned and the evolution of the regulatory and corporate environment, ensuring that its mechanisms
for identifying, assessing, mitigating, and remediating human rights risks are updated.
