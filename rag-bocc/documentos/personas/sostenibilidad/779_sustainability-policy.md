                                      Glossary

ESG: Acronym for Environmental, Social and Corporate Governance matters used
to evaluate impacts, risks and opportunities in organizations.

Sustainable portfolio: Set of financial operations (loans, investments and other
products) that, in addition to meeting financial criteria, incorporate environmental,
social and governance (ESG) considerations into their origination, monitoring and
risk management. This portfolio includes financing that promotes activities with
positive impacts, such as energy efficiency, emissions reduction, biodiversity
protection and social inclusion, and/or that complies with standards and exclusions
defined by the Bank, contributing to responsible risk management and sustainable
development objectives.

Climate change: Significant variation in climate directly or indirectly attributable to
human activities and greenhouse gas emissions.

ESG criteria: Environmental, social and governance variables integrated into the
Bank's strategy, operations and decision-making processes.

Sustainable development: A development model that seeks to meet present needs
without compromising the capacities of future generations.

Diversity, equity, and inclusion (DEI): Principles aimed at promoting inclusive,
diverse, and equitable work and social environments.

GHG emissions: Release of greenhouse gases into the atmosphere, from
operational activities or the value chain.

Sustainable finance: Financial activities that incorporate ESG criteria to promote
positive impacts and manage sustainable-related risks.

Greenhouse gases (GHG): Gases that contribute to global warming, such as CO₂,
CH₄, and N₂O.

GHG Protocol: International methodology for the measurement and management
of greenhouse gas emissions.

Stakeholders: Individuals, organizations, or entities that may be impacted by or
influence the Bank's activities.

Materiality: Process to identify the most relevant ESG issues for the business and
its stakeholders.

Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs): United          Nations global agenda
composed of 17 goals to promote sustainable development by 2030.

UN Global Compact: United Nations initiative that promotes principles related to
human rights, labor, the environment and anti-corruption.

Principles for Responsible Banking (PRB): Framework promoted by UNEP-FI to
align the financial sector with sustainable development and the Paris Agreement.

Climate risk: Risk arising from the physical impacts of climate change or the
transition process to a low-carbon economy.

Physical Risk: Risk associated with extreme weather events or gradual changes in
weather that may affect assets, operations, or businesses.

Transition risk: Risk arising from regulatory, technological, reputational or market
changes associated with the climate transition.

ESG risks: Risks related to environmental, social and corporate governance factors
that may impact financial or reputational performance.

ESRMS (Environmental and Social Risk Management System): System used to
identify, assess and manage ESG risks.

Sustainability: Ability to generate economic, social and environmental value in a
balanced and responsible way in the long term.

Stakeholders: Equivalent term for interest groups.

TCFD: International recommendations for disclosure of risks and opportunities
related to climate change.

TNFD: International framework focused on the identification and disclosure of
nature- and biodiversity-related risks and opportunities.

UNEP-FI: United Nations Environment Programme Finance Initiative that promotes
sustainable finance.

Shared value: A management approach that seeks to generate economic and social
benefits simultaneously.

Corporate governance: A system of rules, practices, and processes by which an
organization is directed and supervised.

                            1. SUSTAINABILITY POLICY


1.1 Overview:

Banco de Occidente and its subsidiaries recognize sustainability as a strategic pillar
for long-term value creation and for the responsible management of the economic,
social, environmental and governance impacts arising from their operations,
products, services and business relationships.

In line with Grupo Aval's corporate guidelines, the Bank remains committed to the
adoption of responsible business practices that contribute to sustainable
development, business resilience and the well-being of its stakeholders, integrating
ESG criteria into corporate strategy, decision-making and business management.

For Banco de Occidente and its subsidiaries, sustainability represents the ability to
foster economic growth, promote social well-being and responsibly manage natural
resources, while maintaining a long-term perspective and strengthening stakeholder
trust.

The Bank's sustainability strategy is based on the United Nations Sustainable
Development Goals (SDGs), the principles of the Global Compact, the Asobancaria
Green Protocol and other applicable national and international standards, guidelines
and best practices in environmental, social and corporate governance matters.

The Bank will also promote the progressive integration of criteria related to climate
change, ESG risks and nature management into its processes, operations and
business activities, in accordance with the regulatory and strategic evolution of the
organization.


1.2 Objective

To establish the general guidelines for integrating environmental, social and
governance (ESG) criteria into the strategy, operations, processes and decision-
making of Banco de Occidente and its subsidiaries.
Through these guidelines, the Bank seeks to guide the responsible management of
its ESG impacts, risks and opportunities, promoting business resilience and the
generation of shared value for its stakeholders, contributing to the economic, social
and environmental development of the regions where it operates.

1.3 Alignment of the 2030 Agenda

Banco de Occidente and its subsidiaries recognize the United Nations Sustainable
Development Goals (SDGs) as a reference framework for guiding their sustainability
strategy and strengthening their contribution to the country's economic, social and
environmental development.

In accordance with the nature of its operations and strategic priorities, Banco de
Occidente primarily focuses its efforts on the SDGs related to decent work and
economic growth, industry and innovation, climate action, gender equality,
sustainable cities and communities, and partnerships for the goals, among others
that contribute to strengthening a more inclusive, resilient and sustainable economy.

The Bank may conduct periodic prioritization and materiality assessment exercises
to identify the SDGs and targets where its management has the greatest capacity to
contribute and generate impact.

1.4 Alignment with international and national frameworks and benchmarks

Banco de Occidente and its subsidiaries promote the adoption of national and
international ESG best practices and benchmarks with the objective of strengthening
sustainable business management, transparency and value creation for
stakeholders.
1.4.1 Commitments and Memberships

Banco de Occidente and its subsidiaries promote the adoption and implementation
of national and international standards, principles and best practices that strengthen
sustainable business management and the integration of ESG criteria in their
operations, processes and decision-making.
The organization participates in initiatives aimed at strengthening sustainable
finance and the responsible management of the impacts and opportunities
associated with climate, nature and social change, including:
   •   UNEP-FI.
   •   Principles for Responsible Banking (PRB).
   •   Finance for Biodiversity (FfB).
   •   United Nations Global Compact.
   •   Asobancaria Green Protocol.
   •   Universal Declaration of Human Rights.

1.4.2 Reference frameworks and good practices
Banco de Occidente and its subsidiaries integrate environmental, social and
corporate governance (ESG) criteria into their management and decision-making
model, promoting the identification and responsible management of risks and
opportunities associated with their operations, activities and business relationships.
To this end, the Bank takes as a reference national and international standards,
principles and good practices aimed at strengthening sustainability, resilience and
the responsible management of ESG risks and impacts.
With respect to human rights and responsible business conduct, the Bank guides its
actions in accordance with the principles contained in the United Nations
International Bill of Human Rights, the principles of the United Nations Global
Compact, and the Organization for Economic Co-operation and Development
(OECD) Guidelines for Multinational Enterprises on Responsible Business Conduct.
Environmental, social and climate risk management
The Environmental and Social Risk Management System (ESRMS) is the
institutional mechanism for the identification, evaluation, management, monitoring
and control of environmental and social risks associated with the operations defined
within its scope.
Its application must be carried out in accordance with internal and regulatory
guidelines and the best international practices in force, ensuring the traceability,
documentation, and retention of information required for management, monitoring,
audit, and reporting purposes.
The responsible areas must ensure the proper application of the methodologies,
tools and procedures defined for the management of environmental and social risks.
 The Bank will incorporate ESG risk management into the credit cycle and other
financial activities through environmental and social due diligence processes that
include at least:
   •   Identification and assessment of ESG risks in clients, projects and financed
       operations.
   •   The categorization of transactions according to their environmental and social
       risk level.
   •   The definition of mitigation measures, commitments and financing conditions
       where appropriate.

biodiversity-related risks and opportunities, taking into consideration the
recommendations of the Taskforce on Nature-related Financial Disclosures (TNFD)
and other applicable international frameworks.

guidelines for the identification of green, social and sustainable financial products.
This approach seeks to finance activities with a positive environmental and social
impact, while accompanying clients in their transition to more sustainable, resilient
and competitive business models.
The Bank will actively promote the development and financing of solutions, products,
and initiatives that contribute to the transition to a sustainable, low-carbon, and
inclusive economy, integrating ESG criteria into its value proposition and
progressively orienting its portfolio towards activities that generate positive
economic, social and environmental impacts.

Environmental management and climate action
In environmental and climate-related matters, the Bank promotes initiatives focused

1.4.3 Reporting frameworks
Banco de Occidente and its subsidiaries promote the transparent, consistent and
periodic disclosure of information related to sustainability and environmental, social
and corporate governance (ESG) matters, taking as a reference nationally and
internationally recognized standards and reporting frameworks.

and the Sustainability Accounting Standards Board (SASB), and reports on its
progress and management against the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), the
United Nations Global Compact and the S&P Global Corporate Sustainability
Assessment (CSA).
The Bank also participates in trade union and regulatory reports and other applicable
disclosure exercises, continuously strengthening its ESG information measurement,
monitoring and reporting capabilities.
Banco de Occidente shall maintain structured monitoring, measurement and reporting
mechanisms to periodically assess the environmental, social and climate performance of its
relevant activities and operations.

Exclusion List
The Bank has an Exclusion List that defines activities and practices that are not
subject to financing or that are subject to restrictions as part of its environmental,
social and governance (ESG) risk management.
This list is based on applicable national regulations and international standards,
including the IFC Performance Standards, BID Invest Exclusion Lists, the OECD
Guidelines, and multilateral agreements related to human rights, biodiversity and
climate change.
The Exclusion List includes prohibited activities whose financing is restricted for legal
reasons or because they generate unacceptable environmental and social impacts,
as well as activities subject to specific restrictions and evaluation conditions.
Its application will be mandatory within the linking and credit processes defined by
the Bank.

Transactions involving prohibited activities shall be rejected, while those subject to
restrictions shall be evaluated in accordance with the applicable internal guidelines
and the risk management criteria established by the organization.

Banco de Occidente and its subsidiaries promote the transparent and periodic
disclosure of information related to sustainability and environmental, social and
corporate governance (ESG) matters, taking as reference nationally and

Sustainability Accounting Standards Board (SASB), and reports on its progress and
management against the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), the United
Nations Global Compact and the S&P Global Corporate Sustainability Assessment
(CSA). It also participates in applicable trade union and regulatory reports,
continuously strengthening its capabilities for measuring, monitoring and
disseminating ESG information.

1.6 Sustainability Policy

This Policy has been prepared by the Sustainability Management of the Vice
Presidency of Human and Administrative Talent, which will be responsible for
promoting its updating, implementation and dissemination within the Bank and its
subsidiaries, as well as for submitting it for approval by the corresponding bodies.

The Sustainability Management will be responsible for monitoring compliance with
the Policy, coordinating the implementation of the sustainability strategy and
periodically reporting progress and relevant matters to Senior Management and the
corresponding committees.

The areas responsible for the application of the Policy must provide the required
information and provide the tools, processes and mechanisms necessary for its
compliance.

The content of this Policy will be reviewed periodically or when deemed necessary,
in accordance with regulatory, strategic or environmental changes.


1.6.1 Sustainability Governance
Board of Directors

The Board of Directors is the highest management body with respect to ESG matters
and will have among its responsibilities:

   •   Approve the sustainability strategy and policy.
   •   Oversee the management of ESG matters, including climate change and
       nature-related risks.
   •   Monitor the implementation of the sustainability strategy.
   •   Promote capacity building and training spaces on ESG matters.

Presidency and Senior Management

The Presidency and Senior Management will have the following responsibilities:

   •   Lead and direct the implementation of the Bank's sustainability strategy.
   •   Incorporate ESG criteria into strategic and business decisions.
   •   Promote policies, guidelines and practices that strengthen sustainability at
       different levels of the organization.

Sustainability Committee

The Sustainability Committee will have the following responsibilities:

   •   Support and oversee the implementation of the sustainability strategy.
   •   Monitor ESG indicators, goals and material matters.
   •   Promote the integration of ESG criteria within the Bank's activities and
       processes.
   •   Strengthen capacities and promote a culture of sustainability throughout the
       organization.

Sustainability Management

The Sustainability Management will have the following responsibilities:

   •   Implement and coordinate the sustainability strategy of the Bank and its
       subsidiaries.
   •   Lead working groups and initiatives associated with ESG issues, climate
       change and sustainability.
   •   Coordinate the monitoring of sustainability indicators, action plans, and
       commitments.
   •   Promote the generation of ESG reports and disclosures for internal and
       external use.
   •   Monitor the implementation of initiatives and guidelines associated with
       sustainability in the different areas and subsidiaries.

Vice-Presidencies and Business Areas

The Vice-Presidencies and responsible areas must:

   •   Integrate ESG criteria into their processes and activities.
   •   Implement initiatives and action plans aligned with the sustainability strategy.
   •   Manage and report information related to material ESG matters associated
       with their activities.

The Risk and Compliance areas will support the implementation of processes and

climate change and nature, as well as the generation of information for decision-
making and related disclosures.

Subsidiaries

The Bank's subsidiaries must develop and implement sustainability strategies and
initiatives aligned with corporate guidelines and periodically report on their progress
and management of ESG matters.

This Policy shall enter into force upon its approval by Banco de Occidente's Board
